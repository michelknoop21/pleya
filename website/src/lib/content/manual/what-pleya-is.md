# What Pleya is

Pleya plays what is on your media server. It holds no films or shows of its own: you point
it at a Plex or Jellyfin server, yours or one somebody shared with you, and everything in
that library becomes watchable, at home or away from it.

![The Pleya home screen on iPhone](/docs-media/home-overview.png)

## Where it runs

Phone and tablet (iPhone, iPad, Android), computer (Mac, Windows, Linux) and television
(Apple TV, Android TV). The interface is the same dark, cinematic one everywhere, adapted
to how you are holding the thing: touch on a phone, pointer on a desktop, a five-way pad
on a remote.

## What you can do with it

- Watch films and shows, and pick up where you stopped, on any device
- Give everyone in the house their own profile with their own history
- Download episodes and films to watch on a plane or a train
- Watch live TV and record programmes, if your server has a tuner
- Find, style and re-time subtitles
- Watch in sync with someone somewhere else, through [Watch Together](/docs/watch-together)
- Request titles your server does not have yet, through [Requests](/docs/requests)

## What it deliberately does not do

Pleya does not track you. Recommendations are computed on the device from what you watched,
and that profile never leaves it. There is no account with Pleya, no analytics call, and
nothing to opt out of.

It also does not cast to a Chromecast. Install Pleya on the TV instead (Apple TV or Android
TV), or drive the TV app from your phone with the [Companion Remote](/docs/apple-tv-and-remotes).

## Two backends, one app

Plex and Jellyfin are different systems, and Pleya speaks both. You can have several servers
connected at once, mixed across the two, and the home screen merges them. A handful of
features exist on one side only, mostly because the other server does not offer them:
subtitle search, metadata editing and recording rules are Plex-only. Where that matters,
the chapter says so.
